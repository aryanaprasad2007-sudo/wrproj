3Fractional shares are the whole reason this account exists

Your $2,000 Webull book stalled on integer math. This one doesn't, and that's not a small detail — it's the unlock.

Whole-share sizing on a small book quietly rounds your position to zero. At a $440 budget on a $335 stock, int(440/335) is 1 share — a 76% position. At $200, int(200/335) is 0. The trade simply doesn't happen, and price accidentally becomes your universe filter.

Robinhood does fractional to 6 decimal places. So your $15.07 buys a real, correctly-weighted 20% position in any name at any price. A $900 stock and a $12 stock are now equally tradeable.

⚠ THE PRICE OF FRACTIONAL: YOU LOSE LIMIT ORDERS

Robinhood allows fractional orders only as type=market with market_hours=regular_hours. That means:

  • No fractional limit orders. You cannot name your price.
  • No fractional pre-market or after-hours fills. Entries happen 6:30am–1:00pm PT, full stop.
  • No resting fractional stop-loss. This is the big one — see §6.

A limit order requires whole shares, which requires the stock to cost less than $15.07. If you ask me for a limit order on a $335 name, I'll tell you why it can't be one rather than quietly converting it to a market order. The choice stays yours.

5One trade, start to finish

This is the exact sequence. Nine steps, three actors. Nothing here is optional.

  1. Get real state before anything else. CLAUDE
    get_accounts to confirm ••••9208 is still agentic_allowed, get_portfolio for live buying power, get_equity_positions for the held list. Never scan against remembered numbers — your book changes.
  2. Scan with those real numbers fed in. PYTHON
    py rh_desk.py --book 75.37 --held CVX,JPM. Same engine, same stop/target math as your paper daily track — a ticket here is the identical trade the audition would take.
  3. Read the book. PYTHON
    cache/rh_desk.json or the desk page. Most days: zero tickets. That's a ~0.4 trades/week system behaving correctly.
  4. Price the gap. CLAUDE
    Signals come off completed daily bars, so the price has already moved. get_equity_quotes for what it costs right now. Compare against the entry reference before you decide anything.
  5. Run the pre-trade review. Always. CLAUDE
    review_equity_order returns Robinhood's own alerts — buying power, PDT, halts, collars. I surface those verbatim, especially warnings. This step is never skipped, even on a $15 order.
  6. See the whole ticket. CLAUDE
    Symbol · shares (6dp) · notional · entry ref vs live · stop · target · $ at risk · anything review flagged. If any of those are missing from what I showed you, ask before saying go.
  7. You decide. ARI
    Out loud, in chat, for this ticket. "Yes" to the last trade is not "yes" to this one.
  8. Order goes in. CLAUDE
    place_equity_order — type=market, market_hours=regular_hours, fractional quantity. One ticket per confirmation.
  9. Confirm the fill. CLAUDE
    What actually filled, at what average price. Market orders don't fill where you looked. Then log the stop and target — because nothing is holding them but the two of us (§6).

6Your stops are not real orders

⚠ THE MOST DANGEROUS THING ABOUT THIS SETUP

The engine's stop and target are levels on a page, not resting orders at the broker. Nothing is sitting at Robinhood waiting to protect you. If a name gaps down overnight, no exit fires.

And you can't just place one: fractional orders are market-only, so a fractional stop-loss doesn't exist as a product. It's not an oversight in your code — the venue doesn't offer it.

So a position on this book is only protected if a human looks. That means:

  • Check while the market is open, because the exit is a market order and market orders need regular hours. A stop breach spotted at 9pm can't be acted on until 6:30am.
  • Closing is a normal confirmed sell — same nine steps, same per-trade confirmation. Nothing about an exit is automatic or expedited.
  • I flag it unprompted. Any time you run /positions or the desk and something is near its stop or target, I say so without being asked.

With 0 open positions right now, this costs you nothing. The first time you fill, it becomes the thing that matters most.

7The rules that don't move

  • Per-trade confirmation, no exceptions. If you say "just buy whatever the system says," I'll still bring you each ticket one at a time. I'll say why once and not argue about it again.
  • No standing approval, ever. Approval doesn't carry from one ticket to the next, or from one session to the next.
  • Nothing scheduled. A desk run that places orders never goes behind a cron, a /loop, or a background task. Scanning on a schedule is fine. Executing is not.
  • Your audition waiver is narrow. You waived the ≥30-closed-trade bar for this account only. That's about which evidence gates a strategy — it was never a waiver of per-trade confirmation, and you haven't asked for one.
  • The paper tracks stay separate. Alpaca and Webull auditions run untouched by this book. Cross-reference them; never merge the records.
  • I don't sell you a trade. A ticket is what the system says, not a forecast. I won't talk you into one or out of one you've decided on. You own the decision; I own whether the numbers are right.

8When it says nothing

This engine takes roughly 0.4 trades per week. Most sessions produce nothing. A scan that returns empty is not a failed scan, a stale cache, or a reason to loosen a filter — it's the system doing its job on a day with no setup.

The 30-year validation behind it (PF 1.47 / 2.03 / 2.45 by decade, 1,090 trades) is a record of patience, not activity. The forward audition on it is still open. Both things are true at once, and the second one is why this book is deliberately small.
