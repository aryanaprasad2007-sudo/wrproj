⚠ THE ONE FINDING THAT SURVIVED EVERY TEST

I measured the sub-$5 universe over four window lengths — 30, 45, 60 and 90 days. Almost every statistic moved. One did not: the median name lost money in all four, from −2.9% at 30 days to −15.3% at 90.

So the universe hands you nothing for free. Every dollar of expected return has to come from selection. That's the bar — not an argument against trading it.

0The number I won't give you

You asked how much you'd have made. I'm not going to invent it, and here's exactly why.

A backtest replays a rule: enter here, exit there, sized like this. You pick penny names through your own AI-assisted research. That's a legitimate way to trade — but it isn't a rule I can replay. There's no recorded decision series to run over the last 30 days.

So if I printed "you would have made $312", that number would come from a stock-picking strategy I invented, credited with returns I assigned to it. It would look like evidence and be fiction. Your own house rule says it plainly: every claim has to come from recorded data.

✧ WHAT I BUILT INSTEAD
  • The null. What the universe did — what a blindfolded pick earned. Your research has to beat this, not zero.
  • The friction bar. What spread costs round-trip, and the win rate that implies.
  • The shot clock. How many round trips a cash account physically allows.
  • The conditional. Ending equity as a function of an assumed hit rate — a dial you set, not a forecast I make.

⚠ THIS IS THE OPTIMISTIC VERSION

The universe is built from names listed today. Any sub-$5 stock delisted, halted into oblivion, or reverse-split out of existence during the window is absent by construction. That's survivorship bias, and it only points one way: real blindfolded results were worse than this.

The hit rate swings from 0% to 38.5% purely on how long you hold — a +30% target simply needs time to be reachable. Any single hit-rate number here is an artifact of the window, and I'm not going to hand you one as if it were a property of penny stocks.

✧ AND A CAVEAT ON TOP OF THAT

All four windows end on the same day and overlap heavily, so they are not four independent tests — they're four views of one stretch of market. That stretch was bad for sub-$5 names. This measures a regime, not a law of nature. Do not read it as "penny stocks always lose."

Worth a section, because it briefly flipped the sign of the answer.

The first run said the universe's mean return was +29.91% and the best name was MVIS at +495.9%. Both were false.

A 1-for-15 reverse split. Your Alpaca daily feed returns unadjusted prices, so the split printed as a 15.8× overnight gain and scored as a +496% winner. In reality the stock went $4.20 → $2.13 afterwards — a holder was down about half.

Same feed, second problem: it's IEX-only. It reported SPWR's 28 Jul volume as 616,282; the real consolidated figure was 40,458,558.

✓ FIXED, AND THE FIX IS REUSABLE

deconstruct_splits() detects corporate actions from the overnight ratio and back-adjusts prior bars — the convention Robinhood's adjusted feed uses, which is what I verified against. It caught MVIS (15.8×) and LAZR (254.8×).

Effect: mean +29.91% → −2.81%. One phantom split was carrying the entire result — a corporate action wearing a return's name.

⚠ A SECOND BUG, IN MY OWN REPORT

The first draft of this page said the window was 30 days but actually measured 30 trading bars = 41 calendar days, while the settlement section below computed sessions from 30 calendar days. Two different windows in one document. Now both derive from one dated cutoff, and the script reports the span it actually used so the label can't drift again. Correcting it moved the median from −3.88% to −2.92%.

4The friction bar

Fractional means market orders only. You pay the spread; you never post inside it.

At a 3:1 payoff with a 10% stop, a winner nets 30% − friction and a loser costs 10% + friction. Solve for where those cancel:

Friction bandRound tripWinner netsLoser costsBreakeven win rate
Tight — RIG, SOUN, SNDL1.0%+29.0%−11.0%27.5%
Typical — AMC, BBAI2.5%+27.5%−12.5%31.2%
Wide — GEVO, WKHS5.0%+25.0%−15.0%37.5%

Measured off your account on 2026-08-17: RIG 0.52%, SOUN 0.71%, SNDL 0.81%, BBAI 0.93%, AMC 2.44%, WKHS 3.97%, GEVO 9.35%. Those were post-close quotes, so intraday is tighter — treat the bands as a range, not a measurement.

⚠ THE TRAP IN THAT TABLE

Cheap and volatile names look like the best opportunity and carry the worst friction. GEVO has the widest spread in the set. The names easiest to get excited about are the ones where the bar is highest.

5How many shots you actually get

••••9208 is a cash account. That caps trade count independently of ideas.

Sale proceeds settle T+1. You may buy with unsettled proceeds, but selling that new position before the original settles is a good faith violation — three and Robinhood restricts you to settled funds for 90 days. Modelling someone who avoids that, each of your 5 slots cycles in hold + 1 days:

Average holdSlot cycleMax round tripsWhat that means
3 days4 days25Active swing pace. Optimistic ceiling.
5 days6 days15The likely real number.
10 days11 days5Position-trading pace.

21 sessions, 5 slots. At a realistic 5-day hold that's about 15 round trips — far too few to separate skill from luck, which is exactly why your paper tracks use a ≥30 closed trade bar.

⚠ WHAT THIS MODEL ASSUMES

Every trade is independent, and every outcome is either +3R or −1R. Neither is quite true: penny names move together, so a bad week hits several slots at once, and real exits land all over the place, not on two clean numbers. Correlation makes the spread of outcomes wider than shown — the tails are fatter in real life. Treat this as the shape of the problem, not a probability quote.

7Where this analysis is weak

Stated plainly, so you can discount it correctly rather than trust it uniformly.

  • The universe is a hand-typed candidate list (40 tickers I chose), filtered to sub-$5 from data. I did not screen every listed sub-$5 name, so the sample reflects my choices as well as the market's.
  • The thin-data filter uses IEX volume as a proxy for liquidity, and IEX is a small share of consolidated tape. Six names were dropped that way — WKHS, CHPT, LAZR, MVIS, BLNK, PSNY — and some may be perfectly liquid in reality.
  • The −10% stop and +30% target are imported from your iAPE engine, which was validated on mega-caps. They're a reasonable yardstick, not your method — you haven't told me what your stops actually are.
  • n is 13–18 names over one overlapping stretch. That is enough to set a bar and nowhere near enough to prove a rule.
  • I tried to have this adversarially reviewed and the review largely failed — 35 of 41 checker agents died on a usage limit. Three findings were verified and refuted; the rest never completed. The two bugs on this page I found myself. Assume more remain.

8What I'd actually do with this

  • Paper the selection first. Alpaca paper is already wired. Log every pick your research produces — entry, stop, target, outcome — for 30 closed trades. That turns "is my research good" into the one number this whole page is missing.
  • Prefer tight-spread names. The same idea executed in RIG or SOUN instead of GEVO moves your breakeven from 37.5% to 27.5% — a 10-point swing in required skill, bought purely by choosing where you trade.
  • Respect the shot clock. 15 round trips is the realistic month, and cash settlement means you wait rather than re-enter.
  • Keep it separate from iAPE. Discretionary penny picks and the 30y-validated daily engine are different strategies with different evidence. Mixing their records makes both unreadable.
  • None of this is a recommendation. I'm not telling you to trade this or avoid it. It's your call and your money; my job was to make sure the numbers are real and the uncertain parts are labelled.
